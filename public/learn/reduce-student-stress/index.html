How to reduce stress at university

By the VENUE team · November 2026

Some stress is normal for students, especially during exams and presentations. But when it builds up, it affects sleep, focus and health. This article offers simple everyday techniques.

Common signs

  • Trouble sleeping, or sleeping too much.
  • Loss of appetite, or eating too much.
  • Difficulty concentrating.
  • Quick anger or sadness.

Simple ways to reduce stress

  • Break big tasks into small pieces. A heavy load looks lighter when divided.
  • Do light exercise. A 20-minute walk can improve your mood.
  • Sleep enough. Seven to eight hours is a good target for most people.
  • Breathe slowly. Breathe in for four seconds, hold for four, breathe out for six. Repeat five times.
  • Talk to someone. A friend, relative or counsellor can help you carry the load.
  • Compare yourself less. Social media shows only other people’s best moments.

When to get help

If these feelings last for several weeks or affect your daily life, seek professional support. Most universities have a counselling office, and you can also see a doctor or a licensed counsellor. If you feel you are in danger right now, contact your local emergency services or someone you trust immediately.

This article is general education and does not replace advice from a doctor.
