How to take good notes in class

By the VENUE team · November 2026

Good notes are not about writing down everything the lecturer says. They are about capturing the key ideas in a way you can use later.

Before the lecture

Look over the topic heading and the slides or chapter. If you arrive with an idea of the topic, you will follow faster and know what to write down.

The Cornell method

Divide the page into three parts: a narrow left column for keywords and questions, a large right section for notes, and a bottom section for a summary. After the lecture, fill in the questions and the summary. This method forces you to review what you wrote.

Mind maps

Put the main topic in the centre, then branch out related ideas. It works well for courses with many connected concepts, such as Biology or Economics.

The outline method

Use headings and indented sub-points. It is simple and quick for lectures that are well organised.

General tips

  • Use your own abbreviations, for example an arrow for “leads to”.
  • Mark parts you did not understand with a question mark, then ask or look them up later.
  • Review your notes within 24 hours of the lecture, when you remember the most.
  • Organise notes by course and date so you can find them at exam time.
