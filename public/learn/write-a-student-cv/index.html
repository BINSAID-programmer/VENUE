How to write your first CV as a student

By the VENUE team · November 2026

No big work experience? That is fine. Employers hiring for internships and entry-level roles know students have little experience. What they want to see is how organised you are and what you have learned.

A one-page CV structure

  1. Contact details: name, phone number, a professional-looking email address and your city.
  2. Short summary: two or three sentences on who you are and what you are looking for.
  3. Education: university, programme, expected graduation year, and relevant courses.
  4. Skills: for example Excel, Python, report writing, public speaking, languages you speak.
  5. Projects and activities: class projects, club leadership, volunteering and competitions.
  6. Work experience: if you have any, even short-term jobs.
  7. Referees: two people with their details, after asking for their permission.

Tips

  • Use action verbs: “organised”, “led”, “analysed”.
  • Give measurable results when you can, such as “organised an event for 120 students”.
  • Adjust your CV for each role you apply for.
  • Use a simple font and a clean layout. One page is enough.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Spelling and grammar mistakes. Ask someone else to read it before you send it.
  • An unprofessional email address, such as a nickname.
  • Untrue information. It is often discovered at the interview.
