How to build a weekly study timetable that works

By the VENUE team · November 2026

A good timetable is one you can actually follow, not one that only looks good on paper. Here is how to build a weekly plan that fits you.

Step 1: Write down the fixed things

Start with lectures, exercise, work (if you have a job), meals, prayer or reflection time, and sleep. Your timetable must work around these.

Step 2: Find the free hours

What is left is your study time. Look for blocks of one to two hours and mark them for study.

Step 3: Share time by weight

Hard courses and courses with more credits deserve more time. Do not split your time equally across all courses.

Step 4: Set small goals

Instead of “study Statistics”, write “finish chapter 3 and do five exercises”. A clear goal tells you when you are done.

Step 5: Schedule a weekly review

Set aside 30 minutes at the end of each week to review what you studied. Reviewing early reduces stress before exams.

Step 6: Leave some slack

Do not fill every hour. Keep two or three empty blocks for surprises or work that ran late.

A sample day

  • 6:00 – 7:00 Wake up and get ready
  • 8:00 – 12:00 Lectures
  • 14:00 – 16:00 Study course one
  • 17:00 – 18:00 Exercise
  • 19:30 – 21:00 Study course two
  • 22:30 Sleep

Try your timetable for one week, then adjust it based on what you learned. No timetable is perfect the first time.
